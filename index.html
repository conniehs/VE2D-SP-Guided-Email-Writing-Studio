It offers both browser and mobile app access, 6 it is convenient for customers. 7, it is more costly 8 it requires an initial setup fee.

P1–2 Language Focus: Opening

Key structures in Paragraphs 1 & 2:

1. Purpose Statement

"I am writing to state my preference... and give reasons for my choice." Clearly states why you are writing without revealing your final choice yet.

2. Introducing Options with "namely"

Use ", namely [Option A] and [Option B]." (never "names") to specify the exact two platforms researched.

3. Neutral Background Only

Use "Regarding City Tickets, ..." and "For TicketEasy, ..." to describe each option objectively before comparing them in Paragraph 3.

4. Grammar Check: Articles

Notice "it is the most popular..." (superlative takes the) versus "it is a well-established..." (singular countable noun takes a).

P3–4 Language Focus: Choice & Closing

Key connectors in Paragraphs 3 & 4:

1. Stating Preference & Reasons

Begin with "After comparison, I would prefer..." Use which to list optional services and so to explain the benefit for customers.

2. Balanced Concession ("On the other hand")

Introduce the non-preferred option with "On the other hand, [Option B] is not preferred." Acknowledge a positive point first (It offers..., so it is convenient...).

3. Contrast & One Concern ("However... as")

Use "However, it is more costly as..." to highlight one key concern from the task rather than every flaw.

4. Polite Workplace Closing

End with a courteous follow-up sentence ("Please feel free to contact me if you have any questions.") and Regards.

Quick Reference Guide

4-Paragraph Email Blueprint

Paragraph 1 · Introduction

State the purpose of writing clearly in one sentence without revealing your choice yet.
"I am writing to state my preference for... and give reasons for my choice."

Paragraph 2 · Background Description

Introduce both options using namely, then describe each objectively using Regarding [Option A], ... and For [Option B], ... (no comparisons yet).

Paragraph 3 · Preference & One Concern

State your preference (After comparison, I would prefer...) and elaborate using which and so. Then introduce the other option with On the other hand, note one strength, and explain one concern using However and as.

Paragraph 4 · Polite Closing

End with a helpful closing sentence ("I hope these ideas are useful to you. Please feel free to contact me if you have any questions.") and sign off with Regards.
